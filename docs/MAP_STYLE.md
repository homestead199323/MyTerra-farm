# MyTerra 3D map — the visual style, locked

**Decided 2026-10-05; look A applied 2026-11-07.** The 3D farm map is a *toy diorama*: rounded, flat-coloured, sunny and warmly lit, in the app's own palette. Look A (2026-11-07) follows the target picture Dervis approved (Gemini "direction A — richer toy", kept outside the repo): warm olive land, clearer and longer shadows, brighter trees, red sheds, a navy night with lit windows and a glowing greenhouse. This file is the single source of truth for how anything on the map should look. Read it before touching `src/features/grove/`. Do not re-open the style question in a session — change this file first if the style changes.

Reference that set the direction: Dilum Sanjaya's "WareTrack" warehouse dashboard (X, 3 Oct 2026) — rounded boxes, one brand colour, white/pale ground, soft shadows, floating white UI cards.

## 0. Two looks side by side (2026-11-10)
Buildings, ground, props and decoration trees follow the toy rules below. **Living things are moving to look C — true to life** (§10), decided by Dervis on 2026-11-10 from side-by-side samples: "more quality and more realistic". Done: every crop, every fruit and nut tree and every animal (2026-11-10, four slices). The decoration trees round the farm and in gardens are still toy models. Where §1–§2 say "chunky", "matte" or "no shine", read that as the rule for everything that is NOT a look-C model.

## 1. Shape
- **Every box is a rounded box.** `rbox()` from `toy.js`, bevel radius ≈ 24 % of the smallest side, capped at 32 cm. Hard-edged `BoxGeometry` only for pieces under ~6 cm (trims, mullions).
- **Every post is a capsule, every rim a torus, every crown a sphere.** No cones for trees, no flat cut-outs, no planes standing in for objects.
- **Chunky proportions.** Fatter posts and rails than life, thick roofs with rounded ridge caps (`pill()`), oversized doors, big round tree crowns. (Animals left this rule on 2026-11-10, see §10.)
- **Smooth surfaces.** High segment counts (spheres 14×10+, cylinders 16+). The only faceted thing allowed is nothing.
- **Real geometry for everything that is a thing.** Crops (`crops3d.js`), animals (`animals3d.js`), props (`props3d.js`). No sprites, no billboards, no camera-facing quads.

## 2. Surface
- **Matte colour with fine shader texture (look A, 2026-11-07).** `flat(color)` — `MeshStandardMaterial`, roughness ≈ 0.88, metalness 0 — plus `texture(mat, kind)` in toy.js: grain painted in the fragment shader from world position, no image files. `meadow` (soft grass mottling on land, property, lawns), `soil` (clods), `gravel` (specks on paths), `roof` (tile/shingle courses parallel to the eaves, each tile a slightly different shade), `wood` (grain streaks), `leaf` (dappled hedge and tree crowns). The finest grain fades with camera distance so far ground never shimmers. Still no photo textures. Trees stand on a soft contact patch (`M.blob`); the mulch under a fruit tree is a crisp flat disc with a lighter rim.
- **Meadow details (look A):** grass tufts, wildflower clumps (4 heads each) and flat pebbles scattered beyond the hedge (70 / 120 on desktop, 30 / 50 on phones); after dark they darken with the land (`nightTint`).
- Among toy-look objects, glass (greenhouse, windows, tractor cab) and water are the only glossy ones; look-C plants carry their own gloss per surface (§10). Glass and water: materials: `MeshPhysicalMaterial`, low roughness, env map.
- Vertex colours carry variety inside one mesh (crop leaves lighter toward the tip, animal coats, ground patches) — never a texture.

## 4. Light
- Hemisphere light: white sky, warm olive bounce `#b3ac78`, intensity 0.66. One warm sun (`#fff0d6`, 2.45) about 45° up in the fixed afternoon, so shadows are clear and a little long (look A).
- **Shadows are clear**: PCF radius 2, shadow intensity 0.97 (0.93 low sun; clouds fade them by at most 30 %). By day the shadow-casting sun keeps its real azimuth but stays 34–58° up (`skyState`), so winter and evening shadows stay short and readable instead of smearing across the field. Every tree also stands on a soft dark contact patch (`M.blob`, radial falloff in the shader, merged by `bake()`).
- **No scene environment map (2026-11-10).** The room map used to light every surface and cost more than half of each frame. Now matte surfaces are `MeshLambertMaterial` (`flat()` picks it when roughness ≥ 0.5, no metal, not physical) and the room map's soft fill is folded into the hemisphere light (`fillLight()` in toy.js: white-blue `[.907,.907,1]` × 5.36 × the sky's `envIntensity`, added to both sky and ground colours; the light's own intensity is then 1). Only glossy materials (glass, metal, water, anything with roughness < 0.5) still get the room map, for reflections alone (`setEnvironment()`; their diffuse room light is switched off in the shader so they are not lit twice). ACES, exposure 1.15.
- What that changed in the picture: surfaces no longer shade slightly differently by compass direction. Mean difference against the old render 2.5–6 of 255 on the harnesses; the balcony plaster and animals read a touch flatter and lighter. `tests/icons-render.jsx` uses the same model; the small pictures in `src/assets/toy/` were NOT re-rendered (a re-render shifts them by 6–14 of 255, mostly on white animals).
- **No post-processing** (no screen-space AO, bloom, depth of field or tilt-shift): tried 2026-11-07, it blurred the map, lost anti-aliasing and blew out the night glow. Edges stay sharp.
- No vignette, no mottle, no cloud-shadow layers, no darkening of the ground anywhere.

## 4b. Live sky and weather (2026-10-14, `sky.js`, `weather-fx.js`)
- The light above is the **fixed afternoon** (`FIXED_SKY`, "Live sky: off" in Settings → Ground & paths). With Live sky on, the sun follows the local clock and the town's latitude (`data.cityGeo`): four presets blended by solar elevation — high (today's look), low (`#ffe2bd`, longer soft shadows), dusk/dawn (`#ffb876`, `#d9c2b2` sky) and night (a `#a9bde0` "moon" from the mirrored azimuth 40° up, `#5a72a6` sky, navy fog `#22355a`). **Night (look A):** the land outside the hedge slides to navy (`nightTint()` in toy.js on the ground, lighter on the property, darker greens on trees and hedge); window glass, the porch lamp and the greenhouse glass (×2, tinted amber) glow warm (`nightGlow(mat, k, rgb)`); **lit windows are amber (2026-11-09):** the pane itself is tinted `[1, .66, .24]` and glows at ×0.55, because ×1.6 on blue glass blew out to white; on an apartment block about half the neighbours' windows are lit (`winLit`) and the rest go navy (`winDark`), ours are always lit; warm light pools lie on the ground in front of the house door and round the greenhouse (`buildGlowPools`, one additive instanced disc, no real lights). Shadows re-render once a minute.
- Today's weather (Open-Meteo current conditions) flattens the light when cloudy or foggy (less sun, more sky, fainter shadows, nearer fog) and brings **toy clouds**: 3–6 merged rounded white blobs at 16–20 m drifting downwind, **never casting shadows**. Rain is pale instanced streaks, snow slow white flakes, both falling through a box round the camera target; none under reduced motion, half as many on phones.
- **Wind** is one uniform (`WIND`: direction the air moves toward, strength 0.15–1 from km/h ÷ 45). Tree, hedge and crop sway scale with it and travel downwind as a gust wave; smoke bends with it; the barn cupola vane turns into it.
- Crop sway converts world wind into each plant's local orientation, with stable variations in flutter and flexibility. Pond waves follow the wind, with surface normals derived from the same wave as the displacement. Neither changes the sky or lighting presets.

## 5. Motion (all in the vertex shader, one shared clock)
- Tree crowns and hedges sway (tops only). Crops sway above 40 cm. Smoke rises, grows and fades. Bees hover. Harvest frames breathe. Pond water ripples gently (×3 in rain). A ripe fruit tree drops a fruit now and then (it rolls, rests, fades).
- **Animals** (`herd-brain.js` + `animals3d.js`): legs decided by a small brain 4×/s — wander, graze/peck, stand, lie down and chew (cows, sheep, pigs, goats; more at night), swim (ducks and geese on a pond touching their run), scatter when the coop is tapped, and roost: poultry walks to the pop door after dark and is hidden inside until dawn. Pose and interpolation stay in the shader; only changed animals' vertex ranges are re-uploaded.
- **Swallows** (5, 3 on phones) loop and bank high over the farm; **butterflies** flutter over herb beds, hives and wild flowers — real geometry, one instanced mesh each, hidden at night, in rain or (butterflies) under 8 °C.

## 6. Growth and status
- Crops are modelled per family and **per stage**: sprout (sown) → seedling → growing → maturing with small green fruit → harvest window with ripe fruit in full colour. Fruit colour appears only in the harvest window; so does the gold frame round a row and the gold ring round a tree.
- Planted instances have a small, deterministic size variation so a row feels organic. Their positions, spacing and stage stay tied to the grower's actual planting; the same plant keeps its variation across preview dates.
- Lettuce-family plants add a folded inner rosette as they grow; radishes have broad leaves and visible root shoulders; tomatoes carry leaves on visible branches; strawberries have three-leaf clusters and low runners. The same growth-stage and detail-tier budgets apply to these models.
- In the growth preview, plants whose stage changes settle into their new size with a short, stable stagger. Backward previews settle from a larger size; reduced motion switches immediately.
- Changing the system reduced-motion preference while the map is open also stops ambient motion and camera inertia, without requiring a page reload.
- A recorded stage remains the source of truth today. A future preview projects from that stage and labels the result as an estimate; it never changes the recorded observation.
- Harvest frame bars are 8.5 % of the frame's shorter side, between 4 and 9 cm (2026-11-09): slim on a trough or pot row, the full 9 cm on a field row.
- Row markers: a wooden pole with a rounded tag in the stage colour.
- Selection: a gold rounded frame. Crop pick: the crop's own colour, lighter.

## 7. UI over the map (`.g3-*` in `quiet.css`)
- Floating panels (growth preview, crop card) never run under the control column; zone labels are clamped inside the view and drop out when their area's centre leaves it.
- The same white cards as the rest of the app: `var(--color-card)`, hairline `var(--color-border)`, `var(--shadow)`-style soft shadow, brand green for selected / pressed, orange job badges. Dark mode follows the tokens. Never dark translucent pills.
- Zone name pills hang 2 px above the **front edge** of the footprint (the edge nearest the viewer), never on a roof. Labels include small matching line icons, except numbered beds, which stay compact. Placement is `labels.js`: selected → pending jobs → biggest footprint; neighbouring labels can shift one row to avoid a collision, and a viewport-sized budget prevents dense farms from being covered. Far out (`ls < .8`) a zone needs 28 px of screen width for a name, 64 px on phones; selected and job-bearing zones take priority. The area selector can focus any area, including one whose label is hidden.
- A thin white footprint follows a mouse hover or keyboard focus, showing which area can be opened. The selected area retains its gold frame; neither effect changes the scene's lighting.
- Hovering a planted row adds a gold row outline and a compact crop card with its stage and area; pointer feedback distinguishes selectable areas from the ground.
- Selecting a planted row focuses its bed and shows the crop's stage-specific picture. An area selection briefly settles into its gold outline; reduced motion displays it immediately.
- Inspection framing fits the actual zone and crop bounds through the perspective camera, including tall plants and roofs. It preserves the current orbit direction and raises low views to 60° for plant areas. Small pots can be inspected at the same close distance on large farms and balconies; Reset still returns to the whole property. This fit reserves space around the object, but does not yet avoid every overlapping information panel or neighbouring building.

- **Area card (2026-11-09, `ZoneSheet.jsx`, `cover.js`):** tapping an area opens its card without hiding the bed. Phones and windows under 760 px: a bottom sheet 52 % of the screen high; the handle (tap, or a 36 px drag) pulls it up to full height and back. Wider windows: a panel 400 px wide down the right side. It is not modal — the map stays live and tapping another area swaps the card — and it closes with its X or Escape only, never by a swipe or a tap outside. The card reports the part of the screen it covers; `FarmControls.focusZone` then frames the area (fill 0.8) in what is left, also clear of the phone's bars. A sheet pulled up over more than 78 % of the map does not reframe it. The job pop-up (`ZoneTaskPopup`) stays a small centred card.
- **Area chip:** the area selector is a white pill with a chevron (top-left; top row beside the close button in full screen) opening the app's own menu — the whole space first, then every area with an orange job count. No native `<select>`. The first-visit hint and the crop card sit below the chip, so the crop card no longer covers the close button on phones.
- **Planting stages in the card:** each planting shows its own five stage pictures in a row with the current stage framed (`.q-growth-steps`), instead of one generic key per crop above the list.

## 7b. Phones (2026-10-25) — coarse pointer only (`isCoarse()` → host class `g3-coarse`; harness `?coarse=1`)
- Inline on the home screen the map is a **live preview tile** (aspect ≥ 4:3): no gestures, no pills, no controls, no scroll hint; an "Open map" pill (`.g3-open`) with a job-count badge; one tap anywhere opens full screen; closing it returns to the whole-farm home view. The render-quality tier (`mobile`: coarse pointer OR short screen) is separate and unchanged.
- Full screen in portrait looks straight down the farm's long axis (θ −90°, `FarmControls.fit` `turn`) so a landscape farm fills the screen's height instead of a 45 % band; Reset returns to it. **Farms only:** a balcony is always seen from outside the building and a garden from its bottom fence toward the house — turned fully side-on, both became a strip of wall or roofs down one edge.
- **Home framing (2026-11-09, `home-view.js`, tested):** the home view is worked out by `homeView()` — pure maths that centres the space in the part of the screen free of the phone's bars (92 px top, 108 px bottom in full screen) and touches the frame on one axis. **Balcony, portrait screen:** seen along its length from outside, turned up to 58° (the angle that spans the most height is picked in 4° steps); a 4 × 1.5 m balcony's floor spans 35 % of the screen height instead of 20 % and the camera stands 20 m away instead of 24 m, so plants are bigger. A wide window keeps the 22° view. **Back garden:** framed on the garden plus its patio (0.8–1.7 m round the fences); the house is cropped by the top of the frame instead of being centred, so roofs no longer take half the picture. **Farm:** unchanged.
- Zone pills on a balcony area 80 cm deep or less hang just in front of it so the planters stay visible. **Small areas (2026-11-09):** any area under 5.5 pill-heights tall on screen gets the same treatment when the ground in front of it is not another area (`labels.js` `below` / `box`); otherwise the pill stays on the area's front edge. "In front" is where the footprint's near side crosses the vertical through its centre, so a strip seen along its length keeps its pill beside it. Desktop keeps the 22° three-quarter view (also for a tall farm on a landscape window: turned).
- Controls are a centred bottom bar (Growth · Reset since 2026-11-09: pinch zooms and the X top-right is the one close control; + · − · Expand stay on desktop); the growth panel sits above it and its legend wraps instead of scrolling.
- Pinch intent lock (`gestures.js`): zoom always applies; rotate needs a ≥ 15° twist with < 8 % scale change; tilt a ≥ 24 px parallel vertical drift; an undecided pinch becomes zoom after 450 ms (review: zoom already applies while undecided, so a long settle costs nothing and keeps slow deliberate twists); the answer is locked until the fingers lift.

## 8. Performance rules that keep the look affordable
- Everything static is merged per material (`bake()`); repeated parts are instanced (fences, pots, tufts, crops, fruit, bees, smoke).
- **Baked geometry is indexed (2026-11-10).** `bakeSource()` keeps one welded, indexed copy of each source shape (position, normal, uv only) and `mergeBaked()` writes the transformed copies straight into one buffer. Rounded boxes inside `Parts` models are welded too (`partBox`). Same triangles, 40–60 % fewer vertices and megabytes; the merged result is never flagged `shared`, so a rebuild frees it (before, each rebuild leaked about three geometries).
- **Materials:** matte = Lambert, glossy = Standard/Physical with the room map (see §4). Do not add `scene.environment` back, and do not give a matte surface a Standard material.
- Measured 2026-11-10 on headless Chrome with a software rasteriser (relative numbers only, not phone frame rates) — frame time / vertices / geometry memory / first build, before → after: balcony 4 × 1.5 m phone 2028 → 560 ms, 179 k → 67 k, 5.1 → 1.9 MB, 265 → 186 ms; garden phone 1608 → 592 ms, 449 k → 196 k, 11 → 3.9 MB, 259 → 217 ms; all-zones phone 2302 → 963 ms, 856 k → 515 k, 15.9 → 6.4 MB, 443 → 331 ms; all-zones desktop 2977 → 1363 ms, 1.48 M → 796 k, 30.8 → 11.6 MB, 563 → 378 ms. Growth-preview rebuild on the all-zones desktop scene 308 → 109 ms. Triangles and draw calls unchanged; two more shader programs.
- Crop models have three levels of detail chosen farm-wide by plant count (>350 plants, >900 plants).
- Budget at the home view: ≤ 200 draw calls, ≤ 800 k triangles on the all-zones harness (every zone type at once, the worst case). 2026-11-07: 181 calls / 797 k desktop.
- **Phone detail tier (2026-11-07):** when the render tier is `mobile`, `setDetail(.45)` (toy.js) builds every sphere, capsule, cylinder, torus and disc, every Parts model and every animal with fewer segments, rounded boxes with one bevel step, and crops one level of detail lighter. Same silhouettes, about half the triangles: 380 k on the all-zones harness (was 787 k). Icons and desktop always build at full detail.
- **Which phones get it (2026-11-09, `detailFor()` in toy.js):** only farms, and gardens packed like one (over 12 areas or 250 plants). A balcony or back garden builds at full detail on a phone — 60–144 k and 218 k triangles on the harnesses — because at .45 tomatoes were hexagons and shrubs octagons. On the farm tier, tree crowns keep at least 10 × 7 segments (`minSeg` / `minRings` on `ball()`): 423 k on the all-zones harness, 185 calls.

## 8b. The flat view and the layout editor (`AerialArtwork.jsx`, `GroveScene.jsx`)
- The same toy style seen straight from above, reading the same `palette.js` colours: flat fills, rounded corners, no photo textures or image sprites, no texture overlays, no sun-tint layer.
- Shadows are soft and faint and fall to the bottom-left, the way the 3D sun's do (`dx < 0, dy > 0`, opacity ≤ 0.2).
- Ground and the property apron, hedge, boundary fence with a green gate and stone pillars, paths with an edge, zone surfaces (soil, pale soil rows, gravel, water with a stone rim, orchard and pasture greens), crops as rounded blobs with fruit only in the harvest window, pots under container plants, animals in the 3D coat colours.
- Labels are the 3D map's white card pills (brand green when selected), job badges are orange with a white rim, selection is a gold frame, edit handles and the grid are brand green.
- SVG text is drawn at real pixel sizes inside a scaled group — never a sub-1 font size (Chrome mis-measures it).

## 8c. Small pictures everywhere else in the app (`src/assets/toy/`, `toy-art.js`)
- Every crop, fruit tree, animal and area picture in lists, cards, popups and the morning walk is **rendered from the 3D map's own models** — same shapes, colours and light — by `scripts/render-icons.mjs` (dev server + Playwright). Re-run it after any change to the models or palette; never hand-draw or import outside artwork.
- Crops: `crop-<family>-<stage>.webp` for stages 2–5 on a soil board sized to the plant; young stages are framed tighter so they still read at 32 px but stay visibly smaller. `crop-planned` / `crop-sown` for stages 0–1. Fruit trees: `tree-<fruit>-<stage>`. Animals: `animal-<species>` (standing, head up). Areas: `zone-<type>` on a rounded meadow tile. Bees use the beehive tile.

## 10. Look C — true-to-life plants and animals (`plantkit.js`, `crops-real.js`, `trees-real.js`, `animals3d.js`; decided 2026-11-10)
- **What it is.** Every crop has its own entry (56 in `REAL`; an unknown name grows as a plain leafy rosette). Each is built the way the plant grows: tomato up a cane with compound leaves and trusses; pepper forking three times with fruit under the forks; salad leaves as a rosette with a heart; herbs as leaf pairs up their stems, needles (rosemary) or divided leaves on long stalks (parsley); strawberry with trifoliate leaves, flowers and berries at the edge; brassicas as wrapper leaves round a head, crown, curd or a stalk of sprouts; carrot, fennel and dill with feathery fronds; onion and garlic as tube leaves over a bulb (the tops flop at harvest), leek as a fan on a white shank; squashes as runners with big leaves on stalks and the fruit on the ground; beans up three canes; maize with cobs and a tassel; grain in tillers that turn gold; sunflower (its head tips up toward the camera more than a real one, so it shows); cane fruit on arching canes; a grape vine trained along a wire; asparagus spears with fern behind. Real leaf blades, not blobs. Still geometry and vertex colour only: no image textures.
- **Surface, per pixel** (`surfaceShader`): every vertex carries `g3s = (kind, gloss, u, v)`. Kind 1 leaf: midrib, side veins, mottling, sunlight glowing through from behind. Kind 2 bark: ridges along the limb, lichen. Kind 3 fruit skin: mottling, pale pores. Kind 0 plain. `gloss` drives the highlight (leaves 0.1–0.55, fruit about 0.9).
- **Material.** `MeshPhongMaterial`, two instances: solid parts (stems, fruit) front faces only, leaf blades double-sided. `PlantKit.build()` puts solids in group 0 and blades in group 1; a mesh takes `[solid, thin]`. Drawing everything double-sided cost about a quarter more per frame. The seedling (stage 1) is the only crop model still built with the plain kit; it uses the solid material with a plain surface.
- **Shade** is baked into the colours (`bakeShade`): leaves near the stem and undersides are darker.
- **Colour.** Real greens, darker and more varied than the toy mint: tomato `#357f3c`, pepper `#2e7a3a`, lettuce `#6fb04a` → `#d2ec98` at the heart, spinach `#2a7235`, chard `#276a33` with `#d2424a` ribs, kale `#3c6b5e`, basil `#3a9440`, sage `#86a488`, rosemary `#4a7a58`. Fruit ripens green (stage 4) → its colour (stage 5).
- **Levels of detail** (`cropTier(count, phone)`, `REAL_Q`): 0 up to 24 plants, 1 up to 90, 2 up to 300, 3 up to 900, 4 beyond; the phone farm tier is one step lighter. A lighter model carries fewer but larger leaves (`PlantKit.big`), so the plant keeps its volume. Triangle budget per plant: 8,000 / 2,300 / 950 / 650 / 650 (enforced in `tests/crops-real.test.js`).
- **Adding a crop:** one entry in `REAL` in crops-real.js (first match wins) and, if its picture should differ from its neighbours, its own entry in `FAMILIES` (crop-families.js) so it gets its own icon slug. Then re-render its small pictures.
- **Measured 2026-11-10** (headless Chrome, software rasteriser, phone tile; relative only, ±10 %; before → after): balcony 4 × 1.5 m 284 → 320 ms, 87 k → 99 k triangles; balcony 11 × 4.5 m 370 → 454 ms, 173 k → 156 k; 36-bed farm 657 → 754 ms, 433 k → 379 k. So look C costs roughly 15 % more frame time where plants fill the view, from leaf blades overlapping each other. Not measured on a phone.
- **Second slice (all remaining crops), same day:** 36-bed farm 657 ms originally → 689 ms, 433 k → 348 k triangles; all-zones farm 393 k triangles, 696 ms; back garden 376 → 396 ms. The old family kits were deleted from crops3d.js.
- **Bundle:** the Grove3D chunk is 231.8 KB gzip. The 230 KB budget is raised to **240 KB** (2026-11-10): splitting the plant models into their own file would not make the download smaller, only two files. Trees and animals will need the question asked again.
- **Known weak points:** on a zoomed-out farm, thin crops (onion, garlic, carrot, grain) leave beds looking emptier than the old chunky models did. Small pictures of fine-leaved plants (thyme, oregano, dill) are harder to read at list size. Okra pods hide in the foliage.
- **Fruit and nut trees (third slice, `trees-real.js`).** One model per species (19 keys), in units of the crown radius; the map scales it by size and stage as before. A barked trunk forking into limbs, a crown of separate leaves over darker inner masses (`PlantKit.lump`), fruit under the foliage at stage 5 only. Habits differ: apple spreads, pear and avocado stand tall, peach and almond are vase-shaped with hanging narrow leaves, citrus is dense and dark, fig is low on three stems with huge leaves, olive is gnarled and silver, hazel and pomegranate are multi-stem shrubs. Trees are drawn through `buildCrops` (items with `tree: true`), one instanced mesh per species, so they sway with the crops. Levels by tree count (`treeTier`): 0 up to 4 trees, 1 up to 10, 2 up to 24, 3 up to 60, 4 beyond; budget per tree 16,000 / 6,500 / 3,200 / 2,200 / 2,000 triangles. The fruit-drop animation and the gold harvest ring are unchanged. All-zones farm after this slice: 396 k triangles, 635 ms on the software rasteriser (696 ms before).
- **Animals (fourth slice, `animals3d.js`).** Same rig, brain and one-mesh herd as before; the models and the material changed. The trunk is one lofted form (`Rig.loft`: stations of half width, top and bottom, slightly square in section) with a chest and, where the species carries one, a belly. Legs are lofted with a knee in front and a hock behind; horses and donkeys stand on one hoof, the rest are cloven. Ears are cupped sheets with a pink inside, manes and tail tufts are hair blades, birds carry tail and wing feathers, three toes forward and one back, a lobed comb. Trunks are leaner and heads smaller than the toy build, and the horse is shorter in the back. **This is simplified anatomy that reads at map size, not a sculpt: no shoulders, no muscle, no face modelling.**
- **Coat material.** `MeshPhongMaterial` with two extra vertex attributes: `g3s = (kind, gloss, patch field, patch cut)` and `g3k = (patch colour, hair scale)`. Kind 4 is coat or plumage, 0 is plain (eyes, hooves, horns, beaks). Breed patches (cow, goat) are a smooth field stored in the vertices and cut per pixel with a little noise, so their edges are crisp at any distance. A stretched noise adds hair or feather grain. Eyes shine (gloss .95), hooves, horns and beaks a little. Undersides are shaded in the colours.
- **Animal levels of detail** (`animalTier(count, phone)`, `ANIMAL_Q`): by the number of animals on the farm — 0 up to 6, 1 up to 16, 2 up to 40, 3 beyond; a phone on a big farm drops one more level. Lighter levels have fewer segments, fewer and wider hair blades, and at level 3 a single hoof. Budget per animal: cattle-sized 7,000 / 4,000 / 2,900 / 2,200 triangles, birds and rabbits 2,200 / 1,500 / 1,150 / 900 (enforced in `tests/animals-real.test.js`). Before this slice a cow was 5,168 triangles at every herd size on a desktop, so herds of seven or more are now lighter than they were; a single animal is heavier (cow 5,988, hen 1,956 against 1,304).
- **Measured 2026-11-10** on the zoo harness (25 animals, phone level, software rasteriser): herd 17.6 k → 28.6 k triangles, frame time 312 → 322 ms, inside the run-to-run noise. Not measured on a phone. Bundle after this slice: Grove3D chunk 237.5 KB gzip of the 240 KB budget — the next addition to the 3D map has to split the chunk or take something out.
